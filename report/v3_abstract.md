# Abstract

Intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) do not fit the classical sequence-structure-function paradigm. Their most-studied functional mechanism is coupled folding-and-binding: a disordered region folds into a defined shape only when it meets its binding partner (a disorder-to-order or d2o transition). Predicting d2o regions from sequence is an open problem. Two data sources could plausibly help: curated Gene Ontology (GO) annotations and pretrained protein language model (PLM) embeddings. Whether they carry independent information has not been tested directly.

I built a dataset of 1,279 non-redundant proteins from DisProt release 2025_12 (188 d2o-positives) and ran a pre-registered 2×2×2 factorial across the three GO Slim sub-ontologies (BP, MF, CC) added on top of an ESM-2 baseline. Cross-validation used GroupKFold on CD-HIT clusters at 40% identity. I tested robustness across three prediction scales (whole-protein, disorder-pool, per-region on 3,231 regions), two PLM families (ESM-2, ProstT5), and two classifiers (Random Forest, XGBoost). Primary metric was AUPRC.

**No GO factor produced a statistically significant lift, but the null is mechanistically informative.** Permutation importance in the joint model splits 99.7% to ESM-2 dimensions and 0.3% to GO features. The 0.3% of GO importance concentrates on biologically correct terms (RNA binding, DNA binding, transcription, nucleus). Modern PLM embeddings have already absorbed the discriminative content that GO Slim carries. This finding generalizes to higher-resolution GO: replacing Slim with full-term ancestor-propagated GO (1,950 BP terms vs 65) gave an initial +0.016 BP lift, but a scrambled-annotation control reproduced 55% of it and the effect vanished across seeds. The strongest lever on prediction is sequence representation, not annotation: baseline AUPRC rises 0.199 → 0.237 → 0.273 across the three scales, each step larger than any GO effect observed. For method developers building IDP function predictors on modern PLMs, engineering better sequence representations returns more than integrating curated context.

**Keywords:** intrinsically disordered proteins; disorder-to-order transition; protein language models; ESM-2; Gene Ontology; DisProt; factorial design.
