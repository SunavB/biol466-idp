# 4. Discussion

## 4.1 Principal finding

The 2×2×2 GO factorial produced no statistically significant main effect or interaction on top of an ESM-2 baseline for predicting the DisProt d2o phenotype.

The general pattern this exemplifies — modern PLMs absorb the discriminative content of categorical annotation features — has been documented before in the broader ML-for-proteins literature (Rives et al., 2021; Elnaggar et al., 2022; Meier et al., 2021) and is expected on prior grounds. **The specific contribution of this study is methodological rather than empirical.** I show that the pattern holds *rigorously and specifically* for the coupled folding-and-binding (d2o transition) phenotype under three conditions that most prior work has not combined: cluster-aware GroupKFold at 40% identity (which blocks the sequence-similarity leakage that inflates most protein-function benchmarks), a pre-registered factorial across three orthogonal GO sub-ontologies (rather than a single GO-versus-no-GO comparison), and a scrambled-annotation negative control (which separates dimensionality-driven Random Forest hedging from true annotation-specific signal at the higher-resolution GO test). The rigor of the demonstration is what is new; the direction of the finding is not.

The mechanistic reading is that **modern PLM embeddings have already absorbed the discriminative content that GO Slim carries at this resolution for this phenotype**. Three lines of evidence support it. The joint model splits importance 99.7%/0.3% in favor of ESM-2 (§3.2). The small fraction of GO importance concentrates on biologically correct terms (RNA binding, DNA binding, transcription regulation, nucleus). And the null holds when I replace Slim with full-term ancestor-propagated GO an order of magnitude finer (§3.5). The scope of the null covers two levels of GO resolution, three prediction scales, two PLM families, and two classifier families.

## 4.2 Why PLMs subsume GO Slim

ESM-2 was trained on approximately 250 million UniRef sequences with masked language modeling (Lin et al., 2023). The training set covers essentially the entire annotated protein universe, including the specific protein families whose Slim GO terms appear at the top of my importance ranking. RNA-binding proteins have distinctive composition biases (Chong et al., 2018); transcription factors share short linear motifs (Van Roey et al., 2014); kinases share catalytic neighborhoods. A PLM at that scale learns these signatures unsupervised. GO Slim's "RNA binding" is a categorical label; the mean-pooled ESM-2 embedding of the same protein is a continuous representation of the sequence features that make it an RNA-binding protein. The two are views of largely the same information, and the continuous view is more discriminative. My factorial does not disprove the value of GO in general. It shows that Slim-level GO cannot beat ESM-2's implicit categorical representation of the same content.

Two auxiliary observations confirm the reading. The ProstT5 replication (§3.6) shows the null generalizes to a PLM from a different family with a structure-aware objective, ruling out ESM-2-specific overlap. The biological coherence of the top-15 Slim features rules out GO annotations being random with respect to d2o.

## 4.3 Regularization toward the class prior

The stratified analysis in §3.3 gives the null an internal structure. GO features lifted AUPRC by +0.028 in the low-confidence tertile and dropped it by −0.031 in the high-confidence tertile. The two effects cancel in the pool. GO context is acting as a Bayesian-like regularizer toward the class prior. When ESM-2 is uncertain, GO features nudge predictions in the direction the biologically correct top terms point. When ESM-2 is confident, the low-resolution categorical signal drags confident predictions back toward the population average. H4 predicted the low-confidence lift; the symmetric high-confidence loss was not pre-registered but fits the same mechanism, and it explains why the pooled average looks flat. This suggests a **conditional GO injection scheme** as a target for future work: apply GO features only where the baseline classifier's predicted probability sits in the low-confidence band.

## 4.4 What the higher-resolution GO test told us

The Week 10 test was meant to disambiguate two hypotheses: either full-term ancestor-propagated GO helps (Slim was too coarse), or it also fails (mechanism generalizes). The strengthening pass (§3.5) revealed a third possibility that pre-registered hypotheses had not considered: **the apparent full-term BP effect was mostly Random Forest hedging**. A scrambled-annotation control reproduced 55% of the lift, and the top-ranked features at full-term resolution are generic ancestor terms rather than IDP-mechanism-specific. The methodological point applies beyond IDP function prediction. Whenever a study adds a large categorical feature set to a strong continuous baseline and observes a small positive lift, a scrambled-annotation control is a low-cost check for this specific failure mode.

## 4.5 Positioning, limitations, future work

**Literature positioning.** CAFA (Radivojac et al., 2013; Zhou et al., 2019) evaluates *sequence → GO* prediction; my study evaluates *sequence + GO → IDPO*. These are different tasks in the same annotation space. A method could improve at the CAFA task while still finding GO adds no information on top of a strong sequence baseline in my task. Kulmanov and Hoehndorf's (2020) DeepGOPlus reports that adding sequence embeddings to GO prediction gave larger improvements than adding better GO representations, which is a direct match. For MoRF prediction, Disfani et al. (2012) and Malhis et al. (2015) report sequence-based AUROCs around 0.75-0.80. Nair et al. (2022) established that cluster-aware evaluation substantially deflates apparent performance in IDP function prediction, which is why my baselines (0.20-0.27) look lower than uncorrected literature benchmarks.

**Limitations.** The sample size (n=1,279, 188 positives) means I can detect a stable +0.02 AUPRC lift but not a stable +0.01 lift. The DisProt-curated slice is better-annotated than average, so my factorial evaluates GO under near-optimal availability. The negative class is d2o-negative-or-unannotated (MNAR), which compresses effect sizes symmetrically but should not distort the relative comparison. GO Slim is coarse by construction; the full-term extension addressed this and the mechanistic story survived, but a semantic embedding of the GO graph (Anc2Vec; Edera et al., 2022) could conceivably rescue a small effect that binary encoding misses. **A scrambled-annotation control would be a necessary component of any such experiment.**

**Future work.** Three directions would give the current null a genuine chance of being overturned: (i) 3-5× more d2o-positive proteins, which requires DisProt curation throughput to grow; (ii) a fine-tuned PLM that incorporates GO context during pretraining rather than as a downstream feature; (iii) conditional GO injection triggered only in low-confidence regions of the baseline predictor.

## 4.6 Biological implications

What does the finding tell us about coupled folding-and-binding as a biological phenomenon in cells? I stay close to what the data support and do not extrapolate beyond it.

**The top-ranked GO features reflect the phenotype's true biological ground.** The Slim features Random Forest ranked highest — RNA binding, DNA binding, transcription regulation, nucleus, cytosol, kinase activity — are exactly the functional and localization categories where coupled folding-and-binding is most prevalent (Wright and Dyson, 2015; Bah et al., 2015; Radhakrishnan et al., 1997). A large share of DisProt's d2o-positives are transcription factors, transcriptional coactivators, RNA-binding proteins, and kinase substrates. The classifier, when given the choice, recognizes exactly the categories a domain expert would call out. This is not a novel finding but it is a useful confirmation that the DisProt curated slice is representative of the biological population underneath the phenotype.

**The 99.7% / 0.3% importance split suggests d2o capability is largely encoded locally in the disordered region's sequence.** If the biological mechanism of d2o transitions were strongly context-dependent — requiring knowledge of the protein's pathway, its subcellular compartment, or its interaction partners to predict — then GO context should have added measurable information beyond the sequence embedding. It did not. Instead, essentially all the discriminative signal lives in ESM-2's representation of the disordered region's own sequence. Biologically, this is consistent with the model of coupled folding-and-binding in which the binding-competent conformation is templated by *local* sequence properties: amino-acid composition biases, short linear motifs at defined register positions, and phosphorylation-site density within the disordered region itself (Chong et al., 2018; Van Roey et al., 2014). Whether the protein is nuclear or cytoplasmic, or which pathway it participates in, appears to be secondary to whether its disordered region carries the sequence signatures that permit a d2o transition. This is a specific biological claim my data support directly: **d2o capability is primarily a property of the local disordered region, not of the protein's broader biological context.**

**The regularization mechanism has a plausible biological correlate.** In the stratified analysis (§3.3), GO features lifted predictions in the low-confidence tertile (+0.028) and hurt them in the high-confidence tertile (−0.031). A plausible biological reading of this pattern: the confident-baseline proteins are those where the disordered region alone gives an unambiguous sequence signature — these are the "textbook" d2o proteins with well-characterized composition biases, such as acidic activation domains of transcription factors or arginine-rich RNA-binding motifs. The uncertain-baseline proteins are those with more subtle or composite signatures where knowing the protein participates in transcription regulation, for instance, provides useful disambiguation. Biologically, this suggests d2o capability sits on a spectrum from strongly sequence-encoded (textbook cases) to weakly sequence-encoded (borderline cases), and the weakly-encoded ones may correspond to less-well-characterized proteins where MoRF-like segments have been proposed but not fully biophysically validated. This is a testable prediction: proteins in the low-confidence tertile should on average have thinner experimental characterization of their disordered regions than proteins in the high-confidence tertile.

**The region-scale CC directional hint could reflect compartment-specific biophysics.** If the region-scale CC main effect (+0.019, BH-adj p = 0.14) and BP×CC interaction (+0.033, BH-adj p = 0.09) are confirmed with more data, they would suggest that certain subcellular compartments create biophysical environments that favor d2o transitions. Nuclear disordered regions operate in dense chromatin and phase-separated condensates where regulatory disordered regions fold upon DNA or partner binding; cytoplasmic disordered regions operate under different mesoscale physical conditions. Whether this translates into a systematic amino-acid-composition difference between nuclear and cytoplasmic d2o regions is a specific hypothesis my data suggest but do not resolve.

**What this study does not support.** I want to be explicit about the scope of the biological claim. The finding is that PLMs subsume the discriminative content of GO Slim for the d2o phenotype in the DisProt curated slice, at n = 1,279 with 188 positives, using two PLM families available in 2023, with binary GO encodings. I cannot claim that GO context is uninformative for IDP function prediction in general, that PLMs subsume all biological context uniformly across phenotypes, or that a different phenotype (e.g. phase-separation propensity, degradation susceptibility) would produce the same null. I also cannot make claims about specific proteins the classifier gets right or wrong — a natural next step would be to look at whether the low-confidence tertile proteins are the ones a wet lab would want to test, since those are the cases where the model is least certain about d2o capability. This report answers a computational question about representation redundancy on this specific phenotype. Extending it to biology requires the follow-up experiments outlined in §4.5.

## 4.7 Conclusion

Modern protein language model embeddings already contain the discriminative content that categorical Gene Ontology annotations carry for predicting disorder-to-order transitions from sequence. This is supported by a 99.7%/0.3% permutation-importance split, biologically coherent top-ranked GO features, the failure of higher-resolution GO to rescue the null (confirmed by a scrambled-annotation control), and the pattern holding across three prediction scales, two PLM families, and two classifier families. The strongest lever on prediction performance is sequence representation, not annotation richness: each step of representation refinement contributes roughly +0.037 AUPRC, larger than any GO effect observed. For method developers building d2o predictors on modern PLMs, engineering effort is better spent on sequence representation and prediction scale than on integrating curated GO context.
