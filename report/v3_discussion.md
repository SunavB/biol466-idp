# 4. Discussion

## 4.1 Principal finding

The 2×2×2 GO factorial produced no statistically significant main effect or interaction on top of an ESM-2 baseline for predicting the DisProt d2o phenotype. That is the surface finding. The mechanistic finding is that **modern PLM embeddings have already absorbed the discriminative content that GO Slim carries at this resolution**. Three lines of evidence support this reading: the joint model splits importance 99.7%/0.3% in favor of ESM-2 (§3.2); the small fraction of GO importance concentrates on biologically correct terms (RNA binding, DNA binding, transcription regulation, nucleus); and the null holds when I replace Slim with full-term ancestor-propagated GO an order of magnitude finer (§3.5). The scope of the null covers two levels of GO resolution, three prediction scales, two PLM families, and two classifier families. That is enough triangulation to make the mechanistic reading the most defensible interpretation.

## 4.2 Why PLMs subsume GO Slim

ESM-2 was trained on approximately 250 million UniRef sequences with masked language modeling (Lin et al., 2023). The training set covers essentially the entire annotated protein universe, including the specific protein families whose Slim GO terms appear at the top of my importance ranking. RNA-binding proteins have distinctive composition biases (Chong et al., 2018); transcription factors share short linear motifs (Van Roey et al., 2014); kinases share catalytic neighborhoods. A PLM at that scale learns these signatures unsupervised. GO Slim's "RNA binding" is a categorical label; the mean-pooled ESM-2 embedding of the same protein is a continuous representation of the sequence features that make it an RNA-binding protein. The two are views of largely the same information, and the continuous view is more discriminative. My factorial does not disprove the value of GO in general. It shows that Slim-level GO cannot beat ESM-2's implicit categorical representation of the same content.

Two auxiliary observations confirm the reading. The ProstT5 replication (§3.6) shows the null generalizes to a PLM from a different family with a structure-aware objective, ruling out ESM-2-specific overlap. The biological coherence of the top-15 Slim features rules out GO annotations being random with respect to d2o.

## 4.3 Regularization toward the class prior

The stratified analysis in §3.3 gives the null an internal structure. GO features lifted AUPRC by +0.028 in the low-confidence tertile and dropped it by −0.031 in the high-confidence tertile. The two effects cancel in the pool. GO context is acting as a Bayesian-like regularizer toward the class prior. When ESM-2 is uncertain, GO features nudge predictions in the direction the biologically correct top terms point. When ESM-2 is confident, the low-resolution categorical signal drags confident predictions back toward the population average. H4 predicted the low-confidence lift; the symmetric high-confidence loss was not pre-registered but fits the same mechanism, and it explains why the pooled average looks flat. This suggests a **conditional GO injection scheme** as a target for future work: apply GO features only where the baseline classifier's predicted probability sits in the low-confidence band.

## 4.4 What the higher-resolution GO test told us

The Week 10 test was meant to disambiguate two hypotheses: either full-term ancestor-propagated GO helps (Slim was too coarse), or it also fails (mechanism generalizes). The strengthening pass (§3.5) revealed a third possibility that pre-registered hypotheses had not considered: **the apparent full-term BP effect was mostly Random Forest hedging**. A scrambled-annotation control reproduced 55% of the lift, and the top-ranked features at full-term resolution are generic ancestor terms rather than IDP-mechanism-specific. The methodological point applies beyond IDP function prediction. Whenever a study adds a large categorical feature set to a strong continuous baseline and observes a small positive lift, a scrambled-annotation control is a low-cost check for this specific failure mode.

## 4.5 Positioning, limitations, future work

**Literature positioning.** CAFA (Radivojac et al., 2013; Zhou et al., 2019) evaluates *sequence → GO* prediction; my study evaluates *sequence + GO → IDPO*. These are different tasks in the same annotation space. A method could improve at the CAFA task while still finding GO adds no information on top of a strong sequence baseline in my task. Kulmanov and Hoehndorf's (2020) DeepGOPlus reports that adding sequence embeddings to GO prediction gave larger improvements than adding better GO representations, which is a direct match. For MoRF prediction, Disfani et al. (2012) and Malhis et al. (2015) report sequence-based AUROCs around 0.75-0.80. Nair et al. (2022) established that cluster-aware evaluation substantially deflates apparent performance in IDP function prediction, which is why my baselines (0.20-0.27) look lower than uncorrected literature benchmarks.

**Limitations.** The sample size (n=1,279, 188 positives) means I can detect a stable +0.02 AUPRC lift but not a stable +0.01 lift. The DisProt-curated slice is better-annotated than average, so my factorial evaluates GO under near-optimal availability. The negative class is d2o-negative-or-unannotated (MNAR), which compresses effect sizes symmetrically but should not distort the relative comparison. GO Slim is coarse by construction; the full-term extension addressed this and the mechanistic story survived, but a semantic embedding of the GO graph (Anc2Vec; Edera et al., 2022) could conceivably rescue a small effect that binary encoding misses. **A scrambled-annotation control would be a necessary component of any such experiment.**

**Future work.** Three directions would give the current null a genuine chance of being overturned: (i) 3-5× more d2o-positive proteins, which requires DisProt curation throughput to grow; (ii) a fine-tuned PLM that incorporates GO context during pretraining rather than as a downstream feature; (iii) conditional GO injection triggered only in low-confidence regions of the baseline predictor.

## 4.6 Conclusion

Modern protein language model embeddings already contain the discriminative content that categorical Gene Ontology annotations carry for predicting disorder-to-order transitions from sequence. This is supported by a 99.7%/0.3% permutation-importance split, biologically coherent top-ranked GO features, the failure of higher-resolution GO to rescue the null (confirmed by a scrambled-annotation control), and the pattern holding across three prediction scales, two PLM families, and two classifier families. The strongest lever on prediction performance is sequence representation, not annotation richness: each step of representation refinement contributes roughly +0.037 AUPRC, larger than any GO effect observed. For method developers building d2o predictors on modern PLMs, engineering effort is better spent on sequence representation and prediction scale than on integrating curated GO context.
